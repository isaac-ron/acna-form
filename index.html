ACNA is not a coalition of organisations that work on behalf of care-experienced people. We are care-experienced people — organising ourselves, defining our own agenda, and building power from within. Every member Network in ACNA is led by the community it serves.

Karibu sana! ACNA is a continental movement by and for care leavers across Africa. Our collective strength comes from Networks that are rooted in lived experience — where those who grew up in care hold the authority, set the direction, and own the outcomes.

For Networks and Organizations only. Applications should be made by an executive with decision-making authority and prior approval from your governing body. The ACNA Management Committee reviews all applications within 6 weeks.

To be eligible, your Network must

  • Be led by care-experienced people — more than half of your leadership must have lived experience of care
  • Bring together care leavers from different homes, institutions, or care backgrounds — not a single placement or facility
  • Ensure leadership itself reflects diversity across different care backgrounds — no single home or institution should dominate
  • Exist to serve and empower care leavers, led by their voices — not on their behalf
  • Be independent of political parties and free from control by external institutions or donors
  • Ensure that no external organisation holds a decision-making position within your leadership
  • Have governing body approval before submitting this application
  • Be committed to a unified, care-leaver-led voice for Africa
  • Alumni groups — care leavers from the same home or institution coming together — do not qualify as member Networks. ACNA is a movement of Networks, not a gathering of alumni.

What membership means for your Network

A real family — across the continent

For many of us, the search for family and belonging did not end when we left care. ACNA is that family — a continental home where care-experienced people find each other, recognise each other, and build something together that is entirely ours. Not a programme. Not a project. A family that understands where you come from, believes in where you are going, and walks with you.

Peer networking forum

Connect with care-experienced Networks across Africa. Share strategies and learn from each other — peer to peer, as equals, at the continental level.

An independent safe space

A space owned entirely by care-experienced people — to speak openly, share challenges, celebrate wins, and define for ourselves what support truly looks like.

Collective resource mobilisation

We partner with member Networks to co-design funding proposals and raise resources together — on our terms, with no power imbalance and no strings attached.

A unified continental voice

Speak as one movement — coordinated, collective, and impossible to ignore. No more isolated Networks navigating the same challenges alone.

Collective engagement with regional & global mechanisms

As an ACNA member, your Network gains access to a structured, coordinated platform for collectively engaging the institutions and processes that shape care policy — at every level. Rather than each Network navigating these spaces alone, ACNA brings care-experienced voices into these rooms together, with a shared agenda and a recognised identity.

Organisational influence & power
Why independence matters

ACNA's mission is to create real power, belonging, and opportunity for care-experienced people across the continent — and to ensure our community is protected from exploitation by external actors. To achieve this, member Networks must be genuinely independent. When external organisations hold decision-making positions within care-leaver Networks, they can — even with good intentions — shape agendas, constrain decisions, and redirect priorities away from what care-experienced people themselves need and want. ACNA exists to change that.

What we are asking

We need to understand whether any organisation — an NGO, INGO, government body, church, or any other institution — holds a formal position of authority within your Network's leadership or governance that could hamper your Network's ability to make free and independent decisions.

A formal decision-making position includes: a board seat, voting rights, veto powers, signatory authority, or any structural role that gives an organisation influence over how your Network is run.
We understand that some Networks began with institutional support. We want to understand the trajectory — where you started, and where decision-making authority is heading.

Why we ask these questions

Among ACNA's core commitments is ensuring that care-experienced people are never exploited or spoken for by external actors. These questions help us understand the landscape of each Network's independence, so we can build something that is truly ours — led by us, accountable to us.

Independence & external affiliations
This includes organisations working within the care reform sector — child welfare bodies, alternative care advocates, deinstitutionalisation programmes, and similar.
A note on care reform organisations specifically. We recognise that many care-experienced leaders work within or alongside organisations in the care reform sector — and that this shared context can be genuinely valuable. It means common patterns are understood, sector dynamics are familiar, and ACNA is better placed to support partners in engaging with us in ways that are meaningful and informed.
At the same time, we ask about these affiliations because decision-making power held by care reform organisations — however well-intentioned — can still shape a Network's priorities, constrain its independence, and blur the line between care-leaver leadership and professional advocacy. We ask not to judge, but to understand — so we can build together with clarity and trust.
We are not asking you to be isolated from the sector — we are asking you to be transparent about how you navigate it.

ACNA requires that all membership applications are submitted with the knowledge and formal approval of your Network's governing body — whether that is a board of directors, a steering committee, a management committee, or an equivalent decision-making authority. Applications submitted without this approval cannot be accepted, and any membership granted on the basis of an unauthorised application may be withdrawn.

ACNA may contact your board chair to verify authorisation. Providing a phone number helps us reach them if needed.
If approval was given verbally or informally, please note that and provide the approximate date.
